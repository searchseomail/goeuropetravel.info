Planning a trip to Europe is an exciting endeavor, but managing your finances while traveling across multiple countries with different currencies can be a headache. One of the most critical mistakes travelers make is using a credit card that charges foreign transaction fees. These fees, typically ranging from 1% to 3% per transaction, can quickly add up, especially when you are calculating how much a 10-day trip to Europe actually costs. By choosing the right travel credit card, you can save money, earn valuable rewards, and enjoy seamless spending throughout the continent.

Why Foreign Transaction Fees Matter

When you use a card that charges foreign transaction fees, every swipe—from your morning croissant in Paris to your museum entry in Rome—costs you extra. If you spend $5,000 during your European vacation, a 3% fee would result in an unnecessary $150 charge. Beyond saving money, the best travel credit cards offer travel insurance, lounge access, and points that can be redeemed for future flights or hotel stays, making your travel budget stretch much further.

Best Practices for Using Credit Cards in Europe

Having a card with no foreign transaction fees is only half the battle. To ensure a smooth experience, follow these essential tips:

  • Always choose the local currency: If a card terminal asks if you want to pay in USD or the local currency (e.g., EUR), always choose the local currency. Choosing USD allows the merchant to set the exchange rate, which is almost always unfavorable.
  • Notify your bank: While many modern banks track location via GPS, it is still a good idea to set a travel notice to avoid any security blocks on your account.
  • Carry a backup: Always carry at least two credit cards from different networks (e.g., one Visa and one Mastercard) in case one is declined or lost.
