How to Meet People and Make Friends While Traveling Solo in Europe

atlas_bound
07.07.2026

Traveling solo through Europe is a rite of passage for many, offering unparalleled freedom and the chance to discover parts of yourself that only surface when you are thousands of miles from home. However, the initial excitement can quickly turn into loneliness if you don’t know how to bridge the gap between being a solo traveler and becoming part of a social circle. Whether you are navigating the historic streets of Central Europe or planning the perfect 7-day Eastern Europe itinerary, the ability to connect with others will undoubtedly enhance your journey.

Choose the Right Accommodation

Where you stay is arguably the single most important factor in your social success. While hotels offer comfort, hostels are the undisputed hubs of solo travel culture. Look for hostels that organize communal activities, such as walking tours, pub crawls, or family-style dinners. These built-in social events remove the awkwardness of approaching strangers by providing a shared activity as a conversation starter.

Hostel Social Hacks:

  • Look for "social" hostels: Read reviews specifically mentioning the common areas.
  • Book a dorm, not a private room: You are far more likely to strike up a conversation with your bunkmate than with a wall.
  • Spend time in the common room: Avoid the temptation to stay in your bed on your phone. Bring a book or a journal to the communal area to appear approachable.

Utilize Technology and Social Apps

Modern travel is supported by a wealth of apps designed to connect like-minded individuals. Platforms like Meetup, Couchsurfing (even if you aren't staying on a couch, the "Hangouts" feature is gold), and various Facebook groups for expats and travelers can help you find local events. If you are exploring the coast, you might find social groups dedicated to island hopping in Croatia where you can find travel buddies for boat trips.

The Art of the "Low-Stakes" Approach

Making friends doesn't require a grand gesture. Often, the best connections start with a simple question. Ask your hostel roommate where they’ve been or if they have any recommendations for coffee shops nearby. If you are worried about safety or potential scams while striking up conversations in crowded tourist spots, it is always wise to read up on how to avoid becoming a victim of scammers in major European cities so you can remain confident and street-smart.

Join Group Activities and Tours

Sometimes, the easiest way to meet people is to pay for a shared experience. Group tours provide a structured environment where interaction is expected. Before you embark on your journey, consider these options:

Activity Type Social Potential Best For
Free Walking Tours High First-day orientation
Cooking Classes Medium-High Foodies and long-term travelers
Pub Crawls Very High Nightlife enthusiasts
Day Trips/Excursions Medium Seeing remote landmarks

Preparation and Mindset

Before you even step on the plane, ensure your logistics are in order. Nothing kills the travel vibe faster than administrative stress, so make sure you understand the visa requirements for your trip well in advance. Once you arrive, keep an open mind. You don't need to find a "best friend" for the entire trip; sometimes, a great conversation over breakfast or a shared sunset is enough to make the memory special.

For those still in the planning phase, make sure to review these 10 essential tips for backpacking across Europe. They cover everything from packing light to navigating public transport, which will give you more mental bandwidth to focus on meeting people rather than worrying about your luggage or train tickets.

Final Thoughts on Solo Travel Connections

Solo travel is not about being alone; it is about being independent. When you are traveling solo, you are more approachable to others who are in the same boat. Smile, be helpful, and remember that everyone in a hostel is likely looking for the exact same thing: a bit of company and a shared adventure. By putting yourself in social environments and staying open to spontaneous invitations, you will find that Europe is not just a collection of beautiful cities, but a network of people waiting to share their stories with you.
