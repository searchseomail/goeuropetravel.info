The 10 Best Cities for Street Food in Europe

cinder_shade
29.06.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Europe is a continent where history, culture, and gastronomy collide on every street corner. While fine dining has its place, the true soul of European cuisine is often found in paper cones, steaming kiosks, and bustling market stalls. From the salty, crispy snacks of the Mediterranean to the hearty, doughy delights of the East, street food offers an authentic window into local life. If you are looking to explore these culinary hotspots on a budget, you might find it helpful to learn how to plan a budget-friendly trip across Europe to maximize your food-tasting adventures.

1. Istanbul, Turkey

Istanbul is arguably the street food capital of the world. Its position as a gateway between East and West has created a unique fusion of flavors. You cannot leave without trying simit (a circular bread encrusted with sesame seeds) or a fresh balik ekmek (fish sandwich) served right off the boats at Eminönü.

2. Naples, Italy

The birthplace of pizza is a haven for those who love fried dough. In Naples, street food is a religion. Look for cuoppo—a paper cone filled with deep-fried seafood or vegetables—and, of course, the legendary pizza a portafoglio (wallet pizza), which is folded so you can eat it on the go. If you are planning to extend your trip to explore nearby regions, consider reading our guide on the most beautiful coastal towns in the Mediterranean.

3. Berlin, Germany

Berlin’s street food scene is a testament to its multicultural identity. While the currywurst remains a quintessential German classic, the city is famous for its massive Turkish influence, specifically the Döner Kebab. If you are a solo traveler navigating these busy streets, check out these safety tips for women traveling alone in Europe to ensure your food tour is worry-free.

4. Budapest, Hungary

Budapest is a dream for foodies who love savory comfort food. The star of the show is lángos, a deep-fried flatbread traditionally topped with garlic water, sour cream, and grated cheese. It is the ultimate late-night snack. This city is a highlight in the perfect 7-day Eastern Europe itinerary that covers the region's best culinary experiences.

5. Palermo, Sicily

Sicily offers a gritty, intense street food culture that dates back centuries. You must try arancini (fried rice balls) and panelle (chickpea fritters). For the adventurous eater, pane con la milza (spleen sandwich) is a local delicacy that defines the street-side culinary spirit of Palermo.

6. Paris, France

While Paris is famous for Michelin stars, its street food—specifically the crêpe—is iconic. Grab a ham and cheese galette from a street vendor near the Seine for a quintessential Parisian experience. If you are looking for a place to stay while indulging in these treats, check out these real recommendations for boutique hotels on a budget.

7. Brussels, Belgium

In Brussels, street food is all about indulgence. The Belgian waffle is a global icon, but don't overlook the frites (double-fried fries) served in paper cones with a dollop of mayonnaise. It is simple, consistent, and undeniably delicious.

8. Madrid, Spain

Madrid’s street food culture revolves around the bocadillo de calamares—a crispy fried squid sandwich served on a crusty baguette. It is the perfect snack to fuel your walk through the city’s historic plazas.

9. Prague, Czech Republic

Walking through the Old Town Square, the scent of trdelník (chimney cake) fills the air. This sweet, grilled dough covered in sugar and cinnamon is the quintessential street snack in Prague, perfect for warming up during a chilly afternoon.

10. Amsterdam, Netherlands

The Dutch have mastered the art of the quick bite. From fresh haring (herring) served with onions and pickles to stroopwafels pressed fresh on the street, Amsterdam offers a mix of savory and sweet that caters to every palate.
