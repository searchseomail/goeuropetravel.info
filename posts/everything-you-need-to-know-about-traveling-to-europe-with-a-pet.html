Pet Travel

Everything You Need to Know About Traveling to Europe with a Pet

urban_wanderer
28.07.2026 • Read time: 4 min

Traveling to Europe with a furry companion is a dream for many pet owners. Whether you are planning to lounge on the sun-drenched coastlines featured in our best European destinations for your summer beach holiday or explore quaint cobblestone villages, bringing your pet requires meticulous planning. Navigating international regulations, airline policies, and local customs can feel overwhelming, but with the right preparation, it can be a seamless and rewarding experience.

Understanding EU Pet Travel Regulations

The European Union has specific entry requirements for pets (dogs, cats, and ferrets) coming from non-EU countries. The rules are designed to ensure public health and animal safety. Before booking any flights, ensure your pet meets the following criteria:

  • Microchip: Your pet must be microchipped with an ISO 11784/11785 compliant 15-digit microchip. This must be done before the rabies vaccination.
  • Rabies Vaccination: Your pet must be at least 12 weeks old at the time of vaccination. The vaccine must be administered after the microchip is implanted, and there is a mandatory 21-day waiting period before travel.
  • EU Health Certificate: You must obtain an EU Health Certificate completed by a government-approved veterinarian in your home country. This document must be issued within 10 days of your arrival in the EU.
  • Tapeworm Treatment: Specifically for dogs traveling to certain countries like Ireland, Finland, Malta, or Norway, a specialized tapeworm treatment must be administered by a vet within 24 to 120 hours before entry.

Choosing the Right Transportation

Getting to Europe is the biggest hurdle. You have two primary options: flying or taking a pet-friendly cruise. When flying, your pet can travel in the cabin (usually only for small pets under a certain weight), as checked baggage, or as manifest cargo.

Method Pros Cons
In-Cabin Pet stays with you Strict weight/size limits
Checked Baggage More space for larger pets Temperature/stress concerns
Cargo Safe for very large dogs Expensive and complex logistics

Pet-Friendly Accommodations and Activities

Europe is generally very pet-friendly. Many hotels, cafes, and even some public transportation systems allow pets. However, always check the specific pet policy of your hotel before booking. If you are planning to visit historic sites or indulge in cultural experiences, such as the ultimate guide to wine tasting tours in France, ensure the venues allow pets beforehand. Many vineyards are outdoors and quite accommodating, but indoor cellar tours might restrict pets.

Tips for a Stress-Free Journey

  • Acclimatization: Get your pet used to their travel crate weeks before the departure date.
  • Vet Visit: Schedule a final wellness check-up to ensure your pet is fit for travel and discuss calming aids if your pet is anxious.
  • Packing Essentials: Bring a familiar blanket, favorite toys, a collapsible water bowl, and enough of your pet’s regular food for the first few days to avoid digestive issues.
  • Hydration: Keep your pet hydrated throughout the flight, but avoid overfeeding right before takeoff to prevent motion sickness.

Navigating European Cities with a Pet

Once you arrive, you will find that European cities are largely walkable. Public transit in countries like Germany, France, and the Netherlands is often accessible to pets, though you may need a muzzle or a dedicated pet ticket for larger dogs. Always carry a copy of your pet’s vaccination records digitally and in physical form. Should you be looking for the best budget-friendly cities to visit in Europe this year, you will find that many of these destinations offer expansive parks and green spaces perfect for pet owners looking to save money while enjoying the scenery.

Traveling with your pet is a significant commitment, but the memories you create together in Europe are incomparable. By staying organized, following the official EU guidelines, and respecting local rules, you and your companion can enjoy the adventure of a lifetime.
