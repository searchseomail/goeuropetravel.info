Experience Europe by Rail: The Most Scenic Train Routes

cipher_logic
19.06.2026 • Read time: 7 min

There is a unique magic to watching the European landscape unfold from behind a panoramic train window. Unlike the disconnected experience of air travel, rail journeys allow you to witness the gradual transformation of cultures, topographies, and climates. Whether you are traversing the snow-capped peaks of the Swiss Alps or winding through the sun-drenched vineyards of the Mediterranean, Europe’s rail network offers an unparalleled way to travel. If you are planning your adventure, be sure to check out these 10 essential tips for backpacking across Europe to ensure your journey is as smooth as it is scenic.

The Glacier Express, Switzerland

Often referred to as the "slowest express train in the world," the Glacier Express is a masterpiece of engineering. Connecting the high-altitude resorts of Zermatt and St. Moritz, this eight-hour journey takes passengers through the heart of the Swiss Alps. You will cross 291 bridges and traverse 91 tunnels, all while enjoying unobstructed views of sheer mountain faces and pristine glacial valleys.

  • Route: Zermatt to St. Moritz
  • Duration: Approx. 8 hours
  • Highlight: The Landwasser Viaduct, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

The Bergen Line, Norway

For those who crave raw, dramatic nature, the Bergen Line (Bergensbanen) is essential. Linking Oslo and Bergen, this line climbs across the Hardangervidda plateau, Europe’s highest mountain plateau. The contrast between the lush forests of the lowlands and the stark, windswept arctic tundra of the summit is breathtaking. It is the perfect route for travelers looking to extend their trip into some of the best European cities for a quick weekend getaway, as Bergen itself offers a stunning harbor and historic charm.

The West Highland Line, Scotland

Famous for being the route of the "Harry Potter" steam train, the West Highland Line offers some of the most rugged scenery in the United Kingdom. As you travel from Glasgow to Mallaig, the landscape shifts from industrial urbanity to wild, untamed glens and deep lochs. The journey across the Glenfinnan Viaduct is a bucket-list moment for many travelers.

Comparing Scenic European Rail Journeys

Planning your rail itinerary requires understanding the different types of experiences available across the continent. Use this comparison table to help decide which route fits your travel style.

Route Primary Scenery Best Time to Visit
Glacier Express Alpine Peaks & Glaciers Winter/Summer
Bergen Line Tundra & Fjords Year-round
West Highland Line Coastal & Highland Spring/Autumn
Bernina Express Glaciers to Palms Year-round

The Bernina Express, Switzerland and Italy

The Bernina Express is a marvel of contrasts. Starting in the chilly, ice-covered peaks of the Swiss Engadin valley, the train descends rapidly through spiral tunnels and over massive stone viaducts, eventually arriving in the palm-lined, Mediterranean climate of Tirano, Italy. This transition happens in just a few short hours, showcasing the incredible diversity of Europe’s geography.

Essential Planning Advice

When budgeting for these iconic routes, keep in mind that luxury panoramic trains often come with a premium price tag compared to standard regional lines. Before you commit to a long-distance rail pass, it is wise to calculate your total expenditures; you can learn more about managing your funds by reading about how much a 10-day trip to Europe actually costs. Regardless of the route you choose, booking in advance is highly recommended, especially during the peak summer months when these scenic lines are in high demand.

Traveling by train in Europe is more than just a means of transportation; it is an immersive experience that connects you to the soul of the continent. By choosing the rail over the runway, you turn a transit day into the highlight of your entire vacation.
